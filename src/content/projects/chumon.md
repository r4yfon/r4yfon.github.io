Ordering food online has become the norm, and it matters most for elderly people who find it physically harder to get out for a meal. But the apps are built for people who grew up with smartphones. Chūmon is a mobile ordering app for a Japanese restaurant, designed to lower that learning curve. I ran it solo as the UX designer, from research to high-fidelity prototype, between May and August 2023.

## Research

I ran interviews, questionnaires and functionality assessments with elderly users, starting with my own family. Three pain points came up again and again:

- **Information architecture:** placing an order or finding a dish in the menu caused confusion and frustration.
- **Poorer vision:** small fonts and unclear elements made the app hard to navigate at all.
- **Low tech-savviness:** unfamiliar patterns made it hard to tell what a feature did.

## Persona and journey

**Christopher, 60**, a supply chain manager who lives with his wife and two children. He wants to be more comfortable ordering online and to try new cuisines with his family. He only speaks Mandarin and isn't tech-savvy.

I mapped [Christopher's ordering journey](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ICwAtBrigB7YXvdznkh8kd5FNtlVBqKMYAJjxjqATVw/edit?usp=drive_link) step by step to find where an elderly user is most likely to give up.

## Wireframes and usability testing

The wireframes drew on the interviews and on design reviews from other designers. A peer usability evaluation then turned up two problems:

1. **Icons travel further than words.** An icon gets its meaning across even when the user doesn't read the label's language, so I added more of them.
2. **The flow didn't guide enough.** Users weren't sure what to do next, so the hi-fi design leads them through ordering one clear step at a time.

## Takeaway

Having grown up with technology at my fingertips, I took its ease of use for granted. Designing for elderly users made me check every "obvious" interaction against someone who has never seen it before. I still apply that test to everything I build.
